Prospector API · v1 · OpenAPI 3

The contract.

Everything Prospector does, for people and robots alike. The web app uses this API with its session; an agent or script uses it with an API key (Authorization: Bearer pk_…) whose scopes say what it may do.

Scopes: read to read; agents:run to start and stop agent runs; agents:configure to add, change and delete the organisation's agents; enquiries:reply to email a reply to an enquiry and record its answer; customers:write to record and change subscriptions and onboardings; customers:invite to email a customer a link to their steps and withdraw it; pipeline:write to work deals and say where answering an enquiry stands; enquiries:triage to say where answering an enquiry stands and put its sender on the board, without deals; enquiries:delete to delete an enquiry; campaigns:write to write campaigns and keep the list of addresses marketing mail never goes to; campaigns:send to send, cancel and resume a campaign; agreements:write to start agreements, upload their documents and set out who signs them and where; enrich:spend to ask the organisation's enrichment vendors, at its cost, for someone's email, phone or profile; people:privacy to export everything held on a person and erase them on their request; settings:write to change the organisation's settings and its enrichment vendors; team:write to add and remove members and change their roles and areas; mail:configure to set the organisation's outgoing mail server; sso:configure to set up single sign-on and its domains; org:export to download everything the organisation holds. A signed-in member may do all of these except configuring agents, deleting enquiries, exporting and erasing people, changing settings, the team, mail or single sign-on, and exporting the organisation, which need an owner.

Signing in, signing out and passwords are account-wide, under /api/v1/ rather than an organisation's address, and only for a browser: a key is refused them. A refusal a client should act on carries a type that names it (see Problem).

Every list comes a page at a time: it takes limit and cursor, and answers {"items": [...], "next_cursor": "..."}. When next_cursor is there, pass it back as cursor for the next page; the Link header names that page too.

Errors

A failure answers with its status and an RFC 9457 problem, whose detail says what to change.

HTTP/1.1 403 Forbidden
Content-Type: application/problem+json

{"type":"about:blank","title":"Forbidden","status":403,"detail":"this key lacks the agents:configure scope"}

Each operation lists the statuses it can answer with. A problem a client can act on without reading detail has a type that links here:

about:blank
Nothing to do but show detail.
/api/problems/signed_out
401: no session or key. Sign in.
/api/problems/session_limited
401: this session began through another organisation's single sign-on. Sign in again.
/api/problems/wrong_login
401: that email and password don't match an account.
/api/problems/throttled
429: too many tries. Wait Retry-After seconds.
/api/problems/link_used
410: a reset link that is used, out of date or replaced. Ask for another.
/api/problems/must_change_password
403: still on a password someone else saw. Choose your own (changePassword) before anything else.
/api/problems/sso_required
403: this organisation requires its single sign-on. Load one of its pages to go through it.
